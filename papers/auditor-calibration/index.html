Calibrating Auditors of Code-Review Claims, and Auditing Their Labels

Benjamin Meadows · Independent Researcher

pdf doi 10.5281/zenodo.24449094 source orcid

Abstract

Automated code review produces claims, and someone has to decide which ones are true. I evaluate language-model agents that re-check a claim against a repository at a fixed commit and report the probability that it is a real, correct finding. I audit the labels as hard as the agents. In a first pass of 61 cases, five of the agent's eight misses turned out to be faults in the evaluation. The final set has 144 cases (52 real findings, 92 false claims) from three sources with different label provenance. Six lanes (a lane is one model in one harness) ran it: four Claude models in one harness, DeepSeek Flash in the same harness, and SWE-2 in a different harness with the files inline and no tools. The area under the ROC curve (AUROC) runs from 0.953 (SWE-2) to 0.994 (Opus 5.5). A rule that knows only where each claim came from, which the lanes were not told, reaches 0.962. Few differences between lanes survive correction for multiple comparisons, and the four that survive every label scenario all involve SWE-2. Giving Haiku 5.5 SWE-2's inputs (files inline, no tools) lowers its recall without lowering its specificity, so missing tools do not explain SWE-2's lower specificity. How the target is defined matters more than most differences between lanes: counting a compound claim as real when any defect it alleges is genuine lowers Opus's AUROC from 0.994 to 0.968, a drop larger than 10 of the 15 pairwise differences. A blind panel of three other models agrees with the frozen labels on 91.0% of cases (Cohen's κ 0.81). All cases come from one maintainer's repositories, and the pairwise tests were chosen after the results were seen, so the comparisons are exploratory.

Code, cases, run data, rulings and this paper's source: https://github.com/TheBenMeadows/auditor-calibration
